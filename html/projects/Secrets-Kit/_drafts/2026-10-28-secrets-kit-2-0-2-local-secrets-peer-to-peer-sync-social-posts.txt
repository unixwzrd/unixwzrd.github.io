Distributed Thinking Systems is preparing Secrets Kit 2.0.2: a substantial re-architecture built around encrypted local SQLite storage, authorized peer-to-peer synchronization, and selected credential access for applications and agents.

The free client supports unlimited peer-to-peer connections on the same LAN segment. Optional Remote Secrets Sync connects peers across networks without making a cloud-hosted vault the source of your credentials. Secret-sync payloads are encrypted for their destination and handled transiently in memory. Your private encryption keys stay with you; we do not receive them and cannot decrypt those payloads.

This is not limited to AI agents. Database clients, scheduled jobs, deployment scripts, and applications that accept environment variables can use the same local process-launch workflow. A process receiving a credential still receives plaintext and can expose it; local storage does not make an application trustworthy.

Final testing is underway. We will announce general availability when qualification is complete. The preview covers the architecture, diagrams, security boundaries, and launch pricing for optional remote connectivity.

Suggested title:
Secrets Kit 2.0.2 preview: local credential storage, encrypted LAN sync, optional remote forwarding

Body:

We're Distributed Thinking Systems, the team behind Secrets Kit. We're preparing version 2.0.2, a substantial re-architecture of the local-first credential tool, and have put together a preview of the storage, synchronization, and application-access model.

The normal new-store workflow uses encrypted SQLite, with each peer keeping its own local storage key. Authorized peers can synchronize directly on the same LAN segment without a hosted subscription. Keychain remains a supported macOS backend, but its items do not automatically become synchronized SQLite records.

For systems across separate networks, optional Remote Secrets Sync connects authorized peers using destination-encrypted envelopes. Your private encryption keys stay with you; we do not receive them and cannot decrypt your synchronized secret payloads. Those encrypted messages are handled transiently in memory, not maintained as a durable cloud secret archive. Account metadata and the separate encrypted invitation exchange have their own storage rules.

Applications receive selected credentials through seckit run. That covers things such as psql, redis-cli, scheduled jobs, and agents. The receiving process gets plaintext and can leak it; this is not a sandbox or protection against a compromised account.

The client and same-LAN peer connections are free. Launch pricing for optional hosted RSS is US$5 per enrolled connection/month, minimum two connections (US$10/month). A connection is an enrolled identity associated with an operating-system user, not every agent or application using it.

This is a pre-announcement. Final testing is still underway; version 2.0.2 installation and production RSS subscriptions are not open yet. The article includes two diagrams and links to the feature, pricing, and security pages.
