Jekyll was driving me nuts. I could write a post, but getting a project into the right menu, page, and update stream took more work than the Markdown. Then there were future posts I could not preview, links I wanted to keep stable, diagrams, audio proofreading, and the checks before and after a push.

I kept solving the next problem. This ten-part series follows what I added: Liquid layouts and includes, YAML catalogs, Python and Ruby utilities, short links under my own domain, local review, deployment checks, and ways for readers to respond. There are Hands-On companions if you want to try the smaller mechanisms yourself. It is still Jekyll; the workflow around it is what grew.
