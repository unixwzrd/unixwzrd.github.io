Asking whether those events were connected was perfectly reasonable. Answering that question requires more than putting the dates next to each other.

I reviewed the advisory, published investigations, operator status histories, and attributed company statements. The article distinguishes the scope of each disruption, reported explanations, and documented downstream effects. It also separates attempted sign-ins from successful access, and shared tooling from evidence of a shared operator.

What concerns me about agent-assisted intrusions is the reduced friction between finding a useful opening and acting on it across multiple targets. That is a practical security problem whether or not any particular outage is eventually connected to ARTEX. Defenders need to understand application identities, credential scope, permission changes, and revocation rather than assume that protecting a human login covers every route into a system.

The article also explains what would persuade me that the outages shared a cause: corroborating incident timelines, compromised suppliers or identities, authentication evidence, and unauthorized changes that explain the failures. A common dependency can matter without there being an attacker at all.
