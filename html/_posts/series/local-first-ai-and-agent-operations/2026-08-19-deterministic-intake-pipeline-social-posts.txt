“Have the model remember everything” sounded like a reasonable next step. The more closely I looked at it, the less I trusted the plan.

Before a model could decide what mattered, I still had to answer the boring questions: What actually exists? What changed? Are these two records the same source? Can I trace a note back to its authority? Will tomorrow's run create the same candidate or another plausible duplicate?

Those are not model problems. They are inventory, identity, state, and provenance problems.

The second article in my Local First AI and Agent Operations series is about moving that work into a deterministic intake pipeline: stable source identity, content fingerprints, explicit review states, retained rejection history, and a separate gate between reviewed knowledge and retrieval. It also explains where the resulting documents actually live: LLM-Wiki is a structured Markdown area inside my broader Obsidian Main Vault, while iCloud is synchronization transport rather than policy enforcement.

The goal was not to remove models from the system. It was to stop asking them to solve bookkeeping and maintenance jobs that ordinary scripts and OS schedulers can handle more reliably.

Title:
Deterministic First: Building a Knowledge Intake Pipeline

Subtitle:
Before a model decides what matters, the system still has to know what exists, what changed, and what a person already decided.

Body:

I reached the point in an agent project where “have the model remember everything” started to sound like the obvious next step. The more closely I looked at the plan, the less I trusted it.

The hard questions came first: stable source identity, content fingerprints, provenance, repeatable reruns, retained rejection history, and a clear boundary between material that has been reviewed and material an agent is actually allowed to retrieve.

This article follows the engineering path that moved those jobs out of model-assisted intake and into deterministic code. It also describes LLM-Wiki inside the broader Obsidian Main Vault, the metadata carried in Front Matter, and the boundary between OS-scheduled maintenance and model-assisted interpretation. Models still have a place, but not where ordinary code can give the system a stronger and more inspectable contract.
