I had plenty of shell scripts that worked, and that was part of the problem.

One script knew how to start a model. Another knew which Python environment belonged to a bridge. A deployment script could copy a working tree to another host. If I was at the right terminal with the recent history in front of me, I could usually put the pieces together.

What the scripts could not do was explain the system after something changed. They did not give me one desired-state authority, show dependency impact before an operation, distinguish intentionally stopped from unhealthy, or tell me exactly what needed to be rolled back after a partial failure.

Part 8 of Local First AI and Agent Operations follows the move from that useful experiment to LLM-Ops-Kit: reviewed service templates, reusable profiles, typed endpoint connections, non-mutating plans, restricted SSH control, durable operation records, and immutable releases.

The article includes a complete inert walkthrough using invented `.invalid` hosts and disabled components, plus diagrams of the configuration and control paths. It has enough detail to inspect the design without asking anyone to point the example at a real model or production machine.

Title:
From Shell Scripts to an Operator-Ready LLM-Ops-Kit

Subtitle:
How I turned a useful collection of scripts into a small control plane that could explain its configuration, dependencies, operations, and recovery.

Body:

I had plenty of shell scripts that worked. That was enough to prove the services could operate together, but it was not enough to make the result understandable after a change or a partial failure.

Part 8 follows the engineering work that came next. LLM-Ops-Kit gives the system one desired-state authority, reviewed service templates, reusable profiles, typed endpoint connections, non-mutating plans, idempotent lifecycle operations, restricted SSH control, durable operation records, and immutable releases with rollback.

The article includes an inert configuration walkthrough with invented hosts and disabled components. It shows how a template becomes a profile and component, how a typed endpoint infers a lifecycle dependency, and how the resulting topology can be inspected without starting a service or pointing the example at a real environment.
